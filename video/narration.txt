Hi, I built TriageLens for the Teens in AI Global Challenge 2027. My question: when a model ranks people for help, who does it miss? It answers in your browser, with no data leaving your device.

I start on the clinic triage scenario with seed 20270101, historical label bias 0.60, and decision threshold 0.35. Every row is synthetic and seeded, with no patient data or real pilot. I train two models from scratch: an L2-regularized logistic regression and a depth-limited Gini decision tree. You see the real loss curve fall and the feature weights behind each prediction.

Now I raise the historical label bias to 0.80 and watch the fairness gap card. The recorded label penalizes rural patients, so the model learns to under-triage them. On held-out rows the card reads twenty point five percent, and the equal-opportunity row reports that the gap increased by seven point one percentage points, not a reduction; the banner does not hide that. Then I move the decision threshold to 0.30 and the card shows fifteen point two percent, unchanged by equal opportunity. I restore bias to 0.60, and at threshold 0.30 equal opportunity cuts the gap from seventeen point one percent to two point four percent. The banner names every recall and secondary-gap cost.

I restore the audited default threshold of 0.35. The cards now read about twenty-four point seven percent before mitigation and twenty-two point four percent after equal opportunity, a reduction of two point three percentage points. At this default, equal opportunity raises overall recall by one point eight percentage points but widens the selection-rate gap by three point six, and the banner reports both. On the scholarship and shelter benchmarks, the same fit cuts the gap from about thirty-seven point seven to six point one percent and from about twenty-eight point seven to five point one percent, while raising recall by five point five and four point three points. Thresholds are fitted on calibrated training scores only, so a small held-out group can be noisy; the residual gap is shown honestly.

I open the counterexample inspector: held-out cases where the global threshold said no support but a mitigation said yes. Each card shows the feature vector and the exact threshold that changed the outcome. Here a fairness metric becomes a human question.

The ethics panel states who benefits, who could be harmed, likely failure modes, and why a human must decide. TriageLens is a teaching instrument, not a clinical tool. It has zero dependencies and works fully offline. Fairness is not a checkbox. It is a measurement you keep making.
